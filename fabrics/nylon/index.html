Nylon

Nylon marks, shines and can melt. Press it only when the instructions with your transfers name nylon. A cotton setting is not a nylon setting.

The rows below are a cautious test, and only if your transfer supplier lists nylon. They are not a published nylon chart. If the packet does not name nylon, do not press it.

Cautious test points, only if your supplier lists nylon
TransferTemperatureTimePressurePeel
Cold-peel standard140°C (284°F)10 secondsLightCold
Warm peel140°C (284°F)10 secondsLightWarm
Hot peel140°C (284°F)8–10 secondsLightHot
Glitter140°C (284°F)10 secondsLightCold

The packet supplied with your transfers wins over this table. Where a named film publishes a different press, the lookup shows that example beside the starting point.

The lookup still shows a test figure, because people ask for one: 140°C (284°F), about 10 seconds, light pressure, cold peel, with a cover sheet. That is a cautious start for a scrap, not a manufacturer’s nylon row. The Xpres guide and the Amaya ProPeel page used on this site name cotton, polyester and blends. They do not name nylon.

Press an inside seam or a spare. Stop if the nylon shines, stiffens or smells. That mark will not wash out. Hot peel is the riskier of the peels here. If the packet says cold, wait, even when you are in a hurry. Glitter on nylon is a test you will often lose. The film has to grip a fabric that does not like the heat the texture wants.

Pre-press only for a moment, under a cover, enough to flatten the panel. A long pre-press can glaze the nylon before the design is even on it. See pre-pressing and shiny marks.

Common questions

Xpres say DTF works on cotton, poly and blends. Is nylon included?

Their how-to says cotton, poly, blends and more. It does not print a nylon temperature or time. More is not a number. Use the sheet that came with your film.

The bag is coated or waterproof.

A coating is a further reason to stop. Heat can mark the coating, and many transfer sheets never claim it. If the packet is silent, do not borrow the cotton row to find out.

Sources

  • Xpres, how to DTF — common press 150–160°C for 10–15 seconds, medium, cold peel
  • Amaya ProPeel DTF film — instant peel; cotton 160°C for 3 seconds, blends 150°C for 10 seconds, polyester 130°C for 15 seconds
