Guide

DTF, HTV and sublimation

They are three different jobs. DTF is a full-colour film, including white. HTV is cut vinyl, usually one colour at a time. Sublimation is a dye that only bonds with polyester.

These are starting points for ready-made DTF transfers. Always follow the instructions supplied with your transfers, then test on a scrap. The number on a heat press dial is often not the true platen temperature.

Which process does which job
DTFHTVSublimation
What it isA printed film you press onCut vinyl you press onDye that becomes a gas and bonds with polyester
CottonYesYesNo
PolyesterYes, with care for shineYes, with care for dye and shineYes, if it is light
White in the designThe film carries white inkThe vinyl is the colourNo white ink
Typical pressAbout 150–160°C for 10–15 seconds for a common cold peelSee the vinyl chartsHotter and longer. See the sublimation charts

The HTV temperatures and the sublimation temperatures are on Heat press settings. This site does not copy those charts across. A common DTF cold peel, from Xpres, is 150–160°C for 10–15 seconds, medium, peeled cold. That is a short press beside a sublimation shirt press.

Choose DTF for a photograph or a full-colour design on cotton, blends, or dark polyester. Choose HTV for a flat name or a simple shape in one colour. Choose sublimation when the blank is light polyester, or a coated item sold for it, and you want the print in the fibre rather than on it.

Plain 100% cotton kids' pyjamas like these have a white chest. It takes DTF and it takes vinyl. It does not take sublimation dye. Heat press settings explains that limit. Pressing longer will not add polyester.

Common questions

Which one should I use for a name on a cotton shirt?

Heat transfer vinyl, if the name is one or two colours. DTF, if the name is full colour or has a fine picture with it. Not sublimation.

Can I press a DTF transfer at a sublimation temperature to make it tougher?

No. Sublimation temperatures are for a different process and a longer time. They are a good way to scorch a shirt and a bad way to apply a DTF film. Use the DTF sheet.
